# Adaptive temporal smoothing: lag against shimmer

Date: 2026-11-14. Status: offline measurement of the new smoothing knobs; not run on the
TV or the LEDs. Platform: macOS, cargo 1.99.0. Processor at `HEAD` = `6dcf4f0` plus the
uncommitted working-tree change that adds **Response** and **Brightness follows**
(`crates/processor/src/nodes/temporal_smoother.rs`). Settings: 162 LEDs (top 51, right 31,
bottom 49, left 31), sample density 128, spatial reach 10 %, black-bar removal off.
User guide: [Picture settings](../../../docs/users/configuration.md#processing).

## Finding

With smoothing at 0.5 s and Response at 100 %, a hard cut is 90 % done on the first
frame for most LEDs (worst LED 100 ms at 30 fps), faster than today's 0.1 s default
(67 ms), while grain moves the LEDs about half as much as today's default does
(dark, blotchy grain: mean 0.06 against 0.17 steps per frame at 30 fps). A fixed 0.5 s
is calmer still (0.04) but takes 367–467 ms to follow a cut. Slow fades and pans are
followed without stepping: the largest frame-to-frame change stays at the unsmoothed
picture's own, and the lights trail the picture by at most 0.42 steps in a fade and
0.67 in a pan (today: 0.12 and 0.27; fixed 0.5 s: 0.77 and 1.95).

Of the brightness variants, **Faster than colour** follows the flash and the cut a little
sooner at low Response but shimmers two to three times as much as **With colour**, because
lightness, where grain mostly is, moves at three times the rate. **Limited** behaves as
**With colour** for grain and fades, and holds a dark-to-bright flash to about 100–130 ms
(the cap of black to white in 0.25 s) where With colour shows it at once.

None of the grain here reaches one just-noticeable step per frame at the LEDs, even
unsmoothed (0.2–0.3 mean, 0.5–0.7 at the 95th percentile): three code values of grain
average away inside each LED's region. The shimmer columns therefore compare settings,
not whether shimmer is visible. Whether flicker of a fraction of a step is seen on a
wall, and which brightness variant looks best, only the TV can tell.

## Rate

Each LED sample moves toward its new colour as `1 - e^(-k · Δt)` of the remaining way per
frame, with Δt the elapsed time, and

- `k(d) = k0 · (1 + β · d / 0.02)`, with `k0 = ln 20 / smoothing seconds` (95 % in the
  smoothing time) and `d` the Oklab distance between the shown and the new colour;
- `β = 1.4 × Response / 100`. At 100 %, with 0.5 s, a change of 10 steps
  (`d = 0.2`) gets `k = 15 k0`, which completes 95 % in one 30 fps frame. The tables
  below kept this value: 100 % follows a cut within about a frame and still stays under
  today's default for grain;
- **With colour**: blend in linear light (Response 0 is bit-for-bit the fixed rate);
- **Faster than colour**: blend in Oklab, lightness at `3 k(d)`, a and b at `k(d)`;
- **Limited**: blend in Oklab at `k(d)`, with lightness changing at most 4 per second
  (black to white, Oklab L 0 to 1, in no less than 0.25 s).

Oklab results are clamped to non-negative linear light. One long frame and many short
ones over the same time no longer give exactly the same colour, because `d` shrinks
during the interval; the unit tests bound the difference below one step.

## Method

[`smoothrun.rs`](smoothrun.rs) builds synthetic 320×180 RGB24 pictures, feeds them to the
real `PrysmProcessor` at exact 30 and 60 fps timestamps, and reads the 162 LEDs as the
WLED output does. Distances are Oklab, in steps of 0.02 (a just-noticeable difference).
Nothing is downloaded; the noise is seeded, so every run is identical.

| Sequence | What | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cut | 1 s warm sunset, then 2 s cool forest | Time from the cut frame until each LED is within 10 % of its starting distance to the forest (90 % done); median and worst LED, LEDs that change by at least one step |
| Flash | 1 s dim room at night, then 2 s screen-filling explosion | As for the cut |
| Grain, bright fine | 5 s still sunset, ±3 code values per 8×8 block per frame (mostly luma) | Frame-to-frame LED change after the first second, mean and 95th percentile |
| Grain, bright blotchy | As above with 32×32 blocks (the blotchy noise of flat compressed areas) | As above |
| Grain, dark blotchy | The dim room with 32×32 blocks | As above |
| Fade | 0.5 s sunset, a 3 s cross-fade in code values to the forest, 0.5 s forest | During the fade: distance to the unsmoothed LEDs (mean, max) and the largest frame-to-frame change |
| Pan | A smooth hue panorama moving half a screen width per second, 4 s | After the first second: as for the fade |

Cut lag is measured in whole frames: 0 ms means the cut frame itself is already 90 %
done.

At 60 fps ([`results.md`](results.md)) the lags agree to within a frame, and every

**Reading the flash rows.** With colour blends in linear light, where a little added
light is a large perceived step, so a dark-to-bright change looks 90 % done sooner than
the same rate in Oklab: at Response 0 the flash takes 233 ms with colour, 367 ms limited
(Oklab at the same rate) and 167 ms faster (Oklab, lightness at three times the rate).
With Response, the size of the flash dominates and only Limited's cap holds it back.

## What the numbers cannot show

- The pictures are synthetic and the grain is a model (uniform block offsets); real film
  grain, compression noise and the TV's own processing were not measured.
- One step (0.02 in Oklab) is a side-by-side figure for colour patches; how visible a
  fraction of a step of flicker is on a wall behind a TV, in the dark, is not known.
- Whether a fast cut or a calm one looks better, and which brightness variant is
  pleasant during an explosion, is a matter of taste on the real TV.
- WLED's own transition and the LEDs' response were not part of this.
